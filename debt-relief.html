Worth knowing

What to weigh before you start

Debt negotiation programs are generally built around unsecured debt of $10,000 or more, and they work by negotiating with creditors over time rather than all at once. That means results depend on your specific creditors and balances — nobody can promise a specific percentage saved before your case has actually been reviewed and negotiated.

It's also worth knowing upfront: pausing payments to creditors while a program negotiates on your behalf can affect your credit and may not stop every collection call right away. By federal law, legitimate debt relief providers can't charge you an upfront fee before they settle or reduce a debt — fees come out of what's actually negotiated. A specialist should walk you through all of this, including whether credit counseling or another path might fit better than negotiation.

Questions

Frequently asked

How much of my debt could be reduced?

We can't promise a number — it depends on your total balance, your creditors, and what they're willing to negotiate. A specialist can give you a realistic picture once they've reviewed your situation.

Will this stop collection calls?

Not automatically, and it's not guaranteed. Some creditors continue contact during negotiation. Your specialist can explain what to realistically expect in your case.

Will my credit be affected?

It can be, especially if payments pause while a program negotiates. Ask your specialist to walk through the specific impact for your situation before you enroll in anything.

Is this a government program?

No. Steady Ledger and the specialists we connect you with are independent, and not affiliated with any government agency.

Is there a minimum debt amount?

Most programs are built around unsecured debt of $10,000 or more. Debt amount is one factor and does not determine qualification — a specialist looks at your full situation, and smaller balances are often better suited to options like credit counseling instead.
